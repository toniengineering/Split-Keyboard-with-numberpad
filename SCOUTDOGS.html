Automated Tier List

Ranks combine the average match performance estimate with match and pit grades. Use tiers to organize scouting evidence—not as a substitute for watching robots.

Scoring model: per-match performance points = (auto hub count × 3) + (teleop hub count × 2) + 15 for a successful climb. Performance score is capped at 60 using a 100-point reference. Average letter-grade GPA contributes up to 40 points. Missing match data does not earn performance points. Grades: A=4, B=3, C=2, D=1, F=0. Tier cutoffs: S ≥85, A ≥70, B ≥55, C ≥40, otherwise D. These are K9.0 scouting estimates, not official event scores.

Lesson 4 · Scouting strategy

K9.0 FRC Scouting Plan & Alliance Selection

Use one consistent process to observe robots, compare teams fairly, catch reliability problems, and recommend partners based on evidence—not reputation or one unusually good match.

K9.0 playbook
01 · Match scouting

Observe each match

Log event, qualification number, team, alliance color, and scouter. Record autonomous actions, teleop scoring, endgame, defense/support role, penalties, downtime, and breakdowns.

02 · Pit scouting

Understand the robot

Ask about mechanisms, intake, scoring options, drivetrain, autonomous routines, endgame capability, strengths, limitations, and repairs. Mark unverified answers as unknown.

03 · Scouting report

Find trends over time

Use averages and match-by-match observations together. Track score variation, tier movement, number of observations, breakdown patterns, and capabilities from pit reports.

04 · Alliance selection

Choose complementary partners

Balance scoring potential with repeatability, uptime, defense/support, endgame, and strategic fit with K9.0. A well-matched role can matter more than a single high score.

K9.0 metrics to connect

MetricWhat scouts should look for
Average performanceAverage scoring actions and final score where available; state how many matches were observed.
ConsistencyScore range or variation across matches, repeatable cycles, and whether the robot can reproduce autonomous actions.
Tier movementCompare saved ranking snapshots; investigate why a team moved rather than treating rank as proof.
ReliabilityBreakdowns, disabled time, missed matches, mechanism failures, recovery time, and repair notes between matches.
CapabilitiesAutonomous routines, intake/scoring mechanisms, drivetrain, endgame, defense, passing/support, strengths, and limitations.
Alliance fitWhich role a team can reliably add, and whether that role complements K9.0 and likely partners.

Alliance partner review

Before recommending a team, discuss each item using observed evidence:

  • Scoring ceiling: what can they score when their robot is working well?
  • Repeatability: do they reproduce their performance over multiple matches?
  • Uptime: how often are they active, and do problems recur?
  • Complementary role: offense, defense, passing/support, autonomous, or endgame.
  • Strategy fit: can the alliance avoid role overlap and coordinate effectively?
  • Confidence: separate verified observations from pit claims or assumptions.
Rule: Do not select on total points alone. Review match samples, reliability, role coverage, and how the partner fits the alliance plan.
Season-specific scoring setup: At the start of each FRC season, read the official game manual and agree on the current season's scoring actions, event-specific labels, and any local weighting used for team comparisons. Keep the scoring profile and assumptions documented. Scout Dog's tier score is a K9.0 decision aid, not an official FIRST ranking or a substitute for the game manual; do not compare scores across seasons without checking that the scoring model is comparable.
